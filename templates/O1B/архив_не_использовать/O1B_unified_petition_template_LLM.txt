0. CORE CONCEPT: WHAT AN O-1B PETITION MUST PROVE
================================================================================

O-1B is not EB-1A and should not be drafted as EB-1A with a different cover page.

There are two possible O-1B tracks:

1. O-1B Arts
The petitioner must establish that the beneficiary:
- has extraordinary ability in the arts demonstrated by sustained national or international acclaim;
- has achievements recognized in the field through extensive documentation;
- is coming to the United States to work in the area of extraordinary ability.

For O-1B Arts, extraordinary ability means “distinction.” In practical drafting terms, prove that the beneficiary is prominent, renowned, leading, or well-known in the field of arts.

2. O-1B Motion Picture or Television (MPTV)
The petitioner must establish that the beneficiary:
- has demonstrated a record of extraordinary achievement in motion picture or television productions;
- is coming to continue work in such productions.

For O-1B MPTV, extraordinary achievement means a very high level of accomplishment in the motion picture or television industry. In practical drafting terms, prove that the beneficiary is recognized as outstanding, notable, or leading in the MPTV field.

LLM INSTRUCTION:
Before drafting anything, classify the case:
[ ] O-1B Arts
[ ] O-1B Motion Picture or Television
[ ] Borderline case requiring explanation why the work is Arts rather than MPTV, or MPTV rather than general Arts

Do not use O-1A language such as “one of the small percentage who have arisen to the very top of the field” for an O-1B Arts petition unless quoting or contrasting law. For O-1B Arts, use distinction / prominent / renowned / leading / well-known. For O-1B MPTV, use extraordinary achievement / outstanding / notable / leading.

Do not use EB-1A language such as “petition for permanent residence,” “Form I-140,” “prospective substantial benefit,” or “final merits determination.” O-1B is a nonimmigrant petition filed on Form I-129. It has a totality review, but not the EB-1A final merits framework.

2. GLOBAL DRAFTING RULES FOR LLM
================================================================================

2.1. Evidence-first writing
Every factual claim must be tied to evidence. Do not write “the beneficiary is internationally recognized” unless the next sentence explains who recognized the beneficiary, for what work, when, and where the evidence is located.

Preferred structure for every claim:
1. Thesis: [Beneficiary] achieved [recognition/result/role].
2. Fact: On [date], [event/publication/organization] did [specific action].
3. Evidence: This is shown by [document type].
4. Citation: (Please kindly refer to Exhibit [X], page [XX]: [document name]).
5. Legal relevance: This supports [criterion element].

2.2. Use O-1B terms precisely
Use “distinction” for O-1B Arts.
Use “extraordinary achievement” for O-1B MPTV.
Use “area of extraordinary ability” for O-1B Arts.
Use “area of extraordinary achievement” for O-1B MPTV.
Use “coming temporarily to the United States” and “events or activities.”

Avoid:
- “permanent residence”
- “I-140”
- “EB-1A”
- “national interest”
- “prospective substantial benefit”
- “labor certification is not required” unless needed for comparison, which is usually unnecessary
- “one of the small percentage at the very top” in an O-1B Arts petition

2.3. Distinguish three different things
Do not merge:
- production/event reputation under Criterion 1;
- organization/establishment reputation under Criterion 3;
- publication/media recognition under Criterion 2 or 4.

These are different evidentiary concepts. USCIS enjoys this kind of taxonomy because apparently ordinary human suffering was insufficient.

2.4. Past and future evidence
Criteria 1 and 3 expressly require both past and prospective work:
- Criterion 1: performed and will perform services as lead/starring participant in productions or events;
- Criterion 3: performed and will perform in lead/starring/critical role for distinguished organizations/establishments.

LLM INSTRUCTION:
For Criteria 1 and 3, always include:
[Past evidence]
[Prospective U.S. evidence]
[Bridge explaining continuity]

If there is no prospective evidence, mark the criterion as risky and do not pretend the requirement disappears because the record is aesthetically pleasing.

2.5. Recommendation letters are not magic dust
For Criterion 5, testimonial letters may be the main evidence. For Criterion 3, detailed letters may help prove critical role. For Criterion 1, unpublished testimonial or recommendation letters are generally not enough because the listed evidence is limited to critical reviews, advertisements, publicity releases, publications, contracts, or endorsements.

2.6. Translations
For every non-English document, include English translation and, where required, a certificate of translation. In the exhibit list, write:
[Document name], English translation, certificate of translation.

2.7. Formatting
Use one exhibit naming system consistently:
Option A: Exhibits
Option B: Attachments
Option C: Annexes
Recommended: Exhibits.

Recommended numbering:
Exhibit 0: General beneficiary documents
Exhibit 0-1: Recommendation / testimonial letters
Exhibit 1: Petitioner and agent documents
Exhibit 2: Consultation / advisory opinion
Exhibit 3: Employment, contracts, itinerary
Exhibit 4: Criterion 1
Exhibit 5: Criterion 2
Exhibit 6: Criterion 3
Exhibit 7: Criterion 4
Exhibit 8: Criterion 5
Exhibit 9: Criterion 6
Exhibit 10: Comparable evidence, if used

2.8. Page references
Do not leave page references as “XX” in the final version. If page references are unavailable at drafting stage, keep [PAGE XX] placeholders and add a production note: “Update after PDF assembly.”

6. SECTION II. CONSULTATION / ADVISORY OPINION
================================================================================

Purpose of this section:
Show that the petition includes the required written consultation/advisory opinion or a legally supported explanation for its absence.

Core rule:
Consultation with an appropriate U.S. peer group, labor organization, management organization, or qualified expert source is generally mandatory before O-1 approval.

For O-1B Arts:
The consultation should come from an appropriate peer group, labor organization, management organization, or person/persons with expertise in the field.

For O-1B MPTV:
Consultation must generally involve the appropriate union representing occupational peers and a management organization in the beneficiary’s area.

LLM INSTRUCTION:
This section is not a recommendation-letter dump. It should prove that the consultation source is appropriate and that the opinion addresses the beneficiary’s qualifications and proposed U.S. work.

8. SECTION III[B]. EVIDENCE OF O-1B ELIGIBILITY
================================================================================

Before writing criteria, select the eligibility path.

Path 1: Significant national or international award / nomination
Use if the beneficiary received or was nominated for a truly significant national or international award in the field, comparable in status to major field-recognized awards.

Path 2: At least three of six criteria
Use in most cases.

Path 3: Comparable evidence
Use only for O-1B Arts when a listed criterion does not readily apply to the occupation and the proposed substitute evidence is genuinely comparable.
Do not use comparable evidence for O-1B MPTV.

8.1. CRITERION 1 — LEAD OR STARRING PARTICIPANT IN DISTINGUISHED PRODUCTIONS OR EVENTS
================================================================================

Regulatory focus:
The beneficiary has performed, and will perform, services as a lead or starring participant in productions or events with distinguished reputations.

LLM ELEMENTS TO PROVE:
1. Past production/event existed.
2. Past production/event had distinguished reputation.
3. Beneficiary’s role was lead or starring.
4. Evidence is in accepted form: critical reviews, advertisements, publicity releases, publications, contracts, or endorsements.
5. Prospective production/event exists or is planned.
6. Beneficiary will perform lead or starring services prospectively.
7. The prospective event/production is distinguished or is reasonably supported by advance publicity, endorsements, reputation of organizers, or similar past productions/events.

Classic examples:
- principal artist in a performance;
- leading actor / singer / dancer / performer;
- headliner at a festival;
- featured artist in a major exhibition;
- principal designer / creative director of a public show;
- key curator or art director where the event is built around their creative concept;
- lead visual / sound / set / costume designer when public materials highlight the role.

Do not use only unpublished recommendation letters here. For this criterion, public-facing evidence matters.

Template:

[B.1] Criterion 1: Evidence that the beneficiary has performed, and will perform, services as a lead or starring participant in productions or events with distinguished reputations

[MR./MS. SURNAME] has performed, and will perform, services as a [lead / starring] participant in distinguished [productions / events] in [FIELD]. The evidence below demonstrates both [his/her/their] past qualifying roles and [his/her/their] prospective U.S. role.

8.2. CRITERION 2 — NATIONAL OR INTERNATIONAL RECOGNITION THROUGH PUBLISHED MATERIALS
================================================================================

Regulatory focus:
The beneficiary has achieved national or international recognition for achievements, evidenced by critical reviews or other published materials by or about the beneficiary in major newspapers, trade journals, magazines, or other publications.

LLM ELEMENTS TO PROVE:
1. There is a published material.
2. It is by or about the beneficiary.
3. It discusses or reflects achievements, not mere biography or advertising.
4. It appears in a qualifying publication.
5. The publication’s status supports national or international recognition.
6. Include title, date, author, and translation if needed.

The publication need not be solely about the beneficiary. It can be about a broader exhibition, production, project, or field topic if it discusses the beneficiary or the beneficiary’s work/achievement and connects the beneficiary to that work.

RISK NOTES FOR LLM:

8.4. CRITERION 4 — MAJOR COMMERCIAL OR CRITICALLY ACCLAIMED SUCCESSES
================================================================================

Regulatory focus:
The beneficiary has a record of major commercial or critically acclaimed successes, shown by title, rating, standing in the field, box office receipts, motion picture or television ratings, or other occupational achievements reported in trade journals, major newspapers, or other publications.

LLM ELEMENTS TO PROVE:

8.5. CRITERION 5 — SIGNIFICANT RECOGNITION FROM ORGANIZATIONS, CRITICS, GOVERNMENT AGENCIES, OR RECOGNIZED EXPERTS

The beneficiary has received significant recognition for achievements from organizations, critics, government agencies, or recognized experts in the field. Testimonials must clearly indicate the author’s authority, expertise, and knowledge of the beneficiary’s achievements.

This is often the most important O-1B criterion for arts cases.

1. The beneficiary has achievements.
2. Recognition comes from a qualifying source: organization, critic, government agency, or recognized expert.
3. The source’s authority is established.
4. The author explains basis of knowledge.
5. The testimonial describes concrete achievements and their significance.
6. The letter is not generic praise.

4. Explanation of why achievements are significant in the field.
5. Explanation of beneficiary’s role in the achievement.
6. If relevant, connection to proposed U.S. work.
7. Signature, title, contact details.

12. VALIDITY PERIOD, EXTENSIONS, AMENDMENTS, AND CHANGE OF EMPLOYER — CLIENT-EXPLANATION BLOCK
================================================================================

Use this outside the petition or in an internal client memo. Do not overfill the USCIS petition with client FAQ unless directly relevant.

Initial validity period:
O-1 petitions may be approved for the time necessary to accomplish the event or activity, up to 3 years, if supported by contracts and itinerary.

Extension:
Extensions are generally available in increments of up to 1 year to continue or complete the same event or activity. There is no simple “automatic renewal.” Each extension requires a new filing and updated evidence.

Change of employer:
A new employer generally must file a new petition. If the original petition was filed by an agent, an amended petition may be needed with evidence relating to the new employer.

Material changes:
Material changes in the terms and conditions of employment or eligibility may require an amended petition. Additional performances or engagements for artists/entertainers may sometimes be added during validity without amendment if they require O-1 caliber services, but this must be checked case-by-case.

Salary and taxes:
Compensation should be real and consistent with the contract. Salary may be annual, project-based, hourly, daily, or another industry-appropriate structure. U.S. tax issues must be handled separately with qualified tax advice. Do not create a petition structure that looks fictional just to make the immigration package prettier. Fiction belongs in screenplays, and even there it should have continuity.

Employer responsibility:
If employment terminates, employer obligations may arise, including return transportation responsibility depending on the circumstances. Avoid fictional arrangements, misleading control structures, or contracts that will not be performed.

15. LLM PROMPT FOR USING THIS TEMPLATE
================================================================================

Use the following prompt to draft a case from raw documents:

You are drafting an O-1B nonimmigrant petition for USCIS. First classify the petition as O-1B Arts or O-1B Motion Picture/Television. Do not use EB-1A or O-1A standards unless explicitly explaining why they do not apply. Use the O-1B standard: distinction for Arts, extraordinary achievement for MPTV. Draft in English. Use Russian only for internal notes if needed.

Inputs:
1. Beneficiary profile
2. Petitioner/agent profile
3. Proposed U.S. employment, contracts, and itinerary
4. Consultation/advisory opinion evidence
5. Evidence by criteria
6. Exhibit index with page numbers
7. Any case-specific strategy notes

Tasks:
1. Identify the correct O-1B track.
2. Identify the strongest eligibility path: significant award, at least three criteria, or comparable evidence for Arts only.
3. Draft the cover letter.
4. Draft Section I: Petitioner / U.S. Agent.
5. Draft Section II: Consultation / Advisory Opinion.
6. Draft Section III[A]: beneficiary overview, field, specialization, biography, and recognition.
7. Draft only the criteria supported by evidence.
8. For each criterion, use the structure: legal element → evidence list → fact narrative → legal relevance → criterion conclusion.
9. Draft Section III[C]: totality conclusion.
10. Draft Section IV: employment, contracts, and itinerary.
11. Draft List of Exhibits and technical divider pages.
12. Mark all uncertain facts as [TO BE CONFIRMED]. Do not invent page numbers, dates, contracts, awards, publications, employers, or compensation.

Quality requirements:
- Every factual claim must have an exhibit reference.
- Do not use generic praise.
- Do not paste entire recommendation letters into the petition.
- Do not claim future U.S. role under Criteria 1 or 3 unless there is evidence of prospective work.
- Do not use comparable evidence for MPTV.
- Do not confuse productions/events with organizations/establishments.
- Do not rely on social media unless it is contextual secondary evidence.
- If publication evidence is used, include title, date, author, publication identity, and translation.
- If salary evidence is used, explain comparison methodology.
- Verify current USCIS fees, forms, addresses, and consultation requirements before filing.
