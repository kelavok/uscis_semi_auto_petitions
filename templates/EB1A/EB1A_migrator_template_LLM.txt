EB-1A MIGRATOR MEMORANDUM TEMPLATE - LLM DISTILLATION

Source template:
templates/EB1A/Шаблон МЕМО EB-1A, ver. 1.2.docx

Purpose:
This template is the EB-1A memorandum style used for the customer labelled
"Мигратор". It is an attorney-style filing package for a Form I-140 EB-1A
self-petition. The drafting flow must preserve the ordinary EB-1A evidence
logic and the system's exhibit hierarchy, but the memorandum order and several
drafting units differ from the base EB1A template.

Global formatting and drafting style:
- Use formal attorney-style English.
- Use clear section headings.
- Keep petition-facing text free of internal drafting instructions, technical
  document IDs, raw filenames, parser notes, placeholders, and script/debug
  comments.
- Use evidence lists and citations in the human-facing exhibit style required
  by the prompt. Technical DOC#### identifiers are only for JSON
  `used_documents` and must not appear in `draft_text`.
- If a document has an English translation, treat the original and translation
  as one human-facing document and add `; English translation` where the final
  exhibit/document list calls for a title.

High-level document order:
1. INDEX:
   - Appears at the beginning of the memorandum.
   - Lists all exhibit groups and documents in the final logical order used in
     the memorandum and bundle.
   - Includes general documents, Industry Overview, each claimed criterion,
     recommendation letters, and documentation establishing prospective
     substantial benefit / continued U.S. work.
2. Attorney cover letter:
   - Date.
   - TO USCIS / USCIS Service Center.
   - RE: I-140 Petition for Alien of Extraordinary Ability.
   - Petitioner, Beneficiary, Citizenship.
   - Attorney-style introduction explaining representation or filing support.
   - Explain that the beneficiary self-petitions under EB-1A.
   - List the claimed EB-1A criteria and direct the officer to the memorandum
     sections and exhibits.
3. OVERVIEW OF THE BENEFICIARY'S QUALIFICATIONS AND ACHIEVEMENTS:
   - Briefly identify the beneficiary, field, specialization, and career
     trajectory.
   - Include work history, education if relevant, achievements, and the role of
     recommendation letters.
   - This section should orient USCIS before the criteria analysis.
4. OVERVIEW OF THE INDUSTRY:
   - Explain the beneficiary's field and specialization.
   - Explain why the specialization is important for the United States.
   - Prefer U.S. government sources, credible institutional reports, major
     media, and industry data.
   - Connect the industry overview to the beneficiary's evidence and future
     U.S. plans without duplicating the later final merits statement.
5. EVIDENTIAL CRITERIA OF ELIGIBILITY:
   - Use only the EB-1A criteria selected for the case.
   - Each criterion should be drafted as a complete USCIS-facing argument.
   - Each criterion should identify the relevant exhibit/list of documents in
     a logical order matching the use of evidence in the text and JSON
     `used_documents`.
6. BENEFICIARY WILL CONTINUE TO WORK IN CLAIMED AREA OF EXPERTISE. FINAL MERIT
   DETERMINATION.
   - Explain the beneficiary's intention and ability to continue working in the
     claimed area of expertise in the United States.
   - Tie the future work to the beneficiary's proven extraordinary ability and
     the industry need / prospective benefit.
   - Use employment plan, business plan, offer, project, collaboration,
     recommendation, and similar documents if present.
7. Conclusions:
   - Concise final conclusion that the beneficiary meets the regulatory
     criteria and merits approval after final merits review.
8. Beneficiary Statement:
   - Separate first-person statement by the beneficiary.
   - Should read like a signable declaration or letter, not like attorney
     argument.
   - Explain the beneficiary's plans, U.S. benefit, and continued work in the
     claimed area.

Criterion-specific template structure:

Criterion 1. Awards
- State the award, year, recipient, organizer, and field.
- Address receipt, recognized status, competitive selection, judging/jury,
  regulations or award rules, prior winners if useful, media/independent
  recognition, and why the award supports excellence in the field.
- Use one episode per award unless the prompt indicates otherwise.

Criterion 2. Memberships
- Identify each association, membership class, admission requirements, and why
  the association requires outstanding achievements judged by recognized
  experts.

Criterion 3. Published material
- Identify the publication/outlet, article title, date, author if available,
  audience/major-media status, and how the material is about the beneficiary
  and relates to the field.

Criterion 4. Judging
- Identify judging activity, event/publication/competition, date, selection
  basis, who or what was judged, and why the judging involved work of others in
  the same or allied field.

Criterion 5. Original contributions
- Separate contribution fact/existence from major significance where the
  workflow uses two phases.
- Identify the contribution, adoption/use, independent corroboration, expert
  support, measurable impact, industry recognition, and why the contribution is
  of major significance.

Criterion 6. Scholarly articles
- Identify publications, venues, dates, field relevance, authorship, and why
  the works qualify as scholarly/professional publications.

Criterion 7. Exhibitions or showcases
- Identify venue, date, work displayed, selection criteria, audience, and the
  significance of the exhibition/showcase.

Criterion 8. Leading or critical role
- Address two elements separately: role importance and distinguished reputation
  of the organization/establishment.
- Use detailed employer/client letters, internal documents, project records,
  product impact, customer/market data, awards, media, and other reputation
  evidence.

Criterion 9. High salary or remuneration
- Identify compensation documents and comparison sources.
- Compare the beneficiary to appropriate position/field comparators.
- Use annual/hourly basis, currency, location, contractor/employee context,
  percentiles/quartiles/ranges, and explain why the compensation is high in
  relation to others in the field.
- The source template includes a salary comparison table with columns:
  Source | Beneficiary Salary | Source Salary | Exceeding (Times).

Criterion 10. Commercial successes in the performing arts
- Use only when the case claims performing-arts commercial success.
- Identify sales, receipts, metrics, market results, distribution, box office,
  record/video sales, or analogous evidence.

Workflow-specific notes:
- The base EB1A `specialization_essay` is not the Migrator template's separate
  section. For Migrator cases, use the `industry_overview` stage instead.
- The separate first-person `beneficiary_statement` stage is required only for
  Migrator cases.
- The exhibit and bundle pipeline remains universal: exhibits are still built
  from validated `used_documents`, exhibit_index.csv, document_index.csv,
  separators, and final bundle assembly.
