EB-1A PETITION TEMPLATE
Unified template based on: (1) working EB-1A structure/notes, (2) actual EB-1A case, (3) USCIS Policy Manual, Vol. 6, Part F, Ch. 2 - Extraordinary Ability.

Purpose:
This template is designed for two audiences at once:
1) a human drafter who needs a clean, repeatable EB-1A petition structure;
2) an LLM agent that must generate a consistent petition without inventing facts, scattering exhibits randomly, or producing an unnecessarily long petition with unfocused citations.

LANGUAGE RULE:
- The petition text submitted to USCIS should be drafted in English.
- Internal drafting comments/rules in this template are in Russian.
- Placeholders are written as __PLACEHOLDER__.
- Remove all drafting comments before final filing.

Every exhibit reference must support a specific factual proposition. Do not dump lists of exhibits without a thesis. The document is not a storage inventory; it is an argument supported by evidence.

1. SOURCE RULES FROM USCIS POLICY MANUAL
============================================================

Use these inserts as drafting anchors. They may be quoted directly in the petition or used as internal instructions. Use only the relevant inserts in the final petition.

1.1. Basic eligibility insert

POLICY MANUAL INSERT:
“Eligibility for Extraordinary Ability Classification
The person has extraordinary ability in the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics, which has been demonstrated by sustained national or international acclaim, and whose achievements have been recognized in the field through extensive documentation.
The person seeks to enter the United States to continue work in the area of extraordinary ability.
The person's entry into the United States will substantially benefit the United States in the future.”

Drafting rule:
The petition must prove three things:
1) sustained national or international acclaim + achievements recognized through extensive documentation;
2) continued work in the same area of extraordinary ability;
3) prospective substantial benefit to the United States.

1.2. Self-petition insert

POLICY MANUAL INSERT:
“A petition filed on behalf of a person with extraordinary ability does not need to be supported by a job offer; therefore, anyone can file the petition on behalf of the person, including the alien who may file as a self-petitioner.”

POLICY MANUAL INSERT:
“The person must still demonstrate, however, that he or she intends to continue work in the area of his or her extraordinary ability and that his or her work will substantially benefit the United States in the future.”

Drafting rule:
Do not overstate “no job offer required” as if nothing else is required. USCIS still expects a concrete, credible work plan.

1.3. Sustained acclaim insert

POLICY MANUAL INSERT:
“When filing a petition for a person with extraordinary ability, the petitioner must submit evidence that the person has sustained national or international acclaim and that the person's achievements have been recognized in the field of expertise.”

POLICY MANUAL INSERT:
“In determining whether the beneficiary has enjoyed ‘sustained’ national or international acclaim, the officer should consider that such acclaim must be maintained. However, the term sustained does not imply an age limit on the beneficiary. A beneficiary may be very young or early in his or her career and still be able to show sustained acclaim. There is also no definitive time frame on what constitutes sustained.”

Drafting rule:
Final merits must show a timeline, not one isolated bright moment. Awards, media, judging, leading role, salary, publications, contributions, and letters should be arranged to show recognition over time.

1.4. Evidence and two-step analysis insert

POLICY MANUAL INSERT:
“This initial evidence must include either evidence of a one-time achievement (for example, a major internationally recognized award, such as the Nobel Prize) or at least three of the types of evidence listed in the regulations.”

POLICY MANUAL INSERT:
“The evidence provided in support of the petition need not specifically use the words ‘extraordinary.’ Rather, the material should be such that it is readily apparent that the person's contributions to the field are qualifying.”

POLICY MANUAL INSERT:
“On the other hand, the submission of voluminous documentation may not contain sufficient persuasive evidence to establish the beneficiary’s eligibility.”

POLICY MANUAL INSERT:
“Officers should use a two-step analysis to evaluate the evidence submitted with the petition to demonstrate eligibility for classification as a person with extraordinary ability.”

POLICY MANUAL INSERT:
“Step 1 Assess whether evidence meets regulatory criteria: Determine, by a preponderance of the evidence, which evidence submitted by the petitioner objectively meets the parameters of the regulatory description that applies to that type of evidence.”
“Step 2 Final merits determination: Evaluate all the evidence together when considering the petition in its entirety for the final merits determination, in the context of the high level of expertise required for this immigrant classification.”

Drafting rule:
Section 1[B] proves the claimed regulatory criteria. The final conclusion synthesizes everything and argues sustained acclaim / top of field. Do not turn the criteria sections into final merits essays too early. USCIS performs both steps; the petition should make both easy.

1.5. Final merits insert

POLICY MANUAL INSERT:
“The evidence provided in support of the petition must ultimately establish that the beneficiary ‘is one of that small percentage who have risen to the very top of the field of endeavor.’”

Drafting rule:
Final merits should not repeat every exhibit. It should explain why the record, viewed together, places the beneficiary at the top level of the field.

2. EVIDENCE MAP AND EXHIBIT ARCHITECTURE
============================================================

Default exhibit architecture:

Exhibit 0. General documents: CV, education, professional certificates, identity-related general materials.
Exhibit 0-1. Support letters from experts in the field, explanatory notes.
Exhibit 1. Criterion i. Awards.
Exhibit 2. Criterion ii. Memberships.
Exhibit 3. Criterion iii. Published material about the beneficiary.
Exhibit 4. Criterion iv. Judging.
Exhibit 5. Criterion v. Original contributions of major significance.
Exhibit 6. Criterion vi. Scholarly articles.
Exhibit 7. Criterion vii. Artistic exhibitions or showcases.
Exhibit 8. Criterion viii. Leading or critical role.
Exhibit 9. Criterion ix. High salary or remuneration.
Exhibit 10. Criterion x. Commercial success in the performing arts.
Exhibit 11. Documents regarding prospective U.S. benefit / employment plan.
Optional Exhibit 12. Additional evidence that does not fit cleanly elsewhere.

Rule:
If a criterion is not claimed, remove it from:
- cover letter criteria list;
- Table of Contents;
- Section 1[B];
- List of Exhibits;
- technical separator pages.

Rule:
Do not renumber criteria in a way that breaks the regulatory numbering. For example, if awards are absent but membership is claimed, membership should still be [B.ii], not [B.i]. Exhibit numbers may remain sequential, but criterion labels must track the regulation.

Recommended exhibit numbering:
- Keep regulatory criterion numbering aligned where possible:
  Exhibit 1 = Criterion i
  Exhibit 2 = Criterion ii
  etc.
This makes the file easier to review and reduces citation errors.

Document cluster rule:
Within each criterion, group documents by legal thesis, not by random upload order.

Example:
1.1. All-Russian “Business Breakthrough 2024” Award
1.1.1. Award diploma, English translation, photos with award, explanatory note.
1.1.2. Official competition website listing beneficiary as winner, English translation.
1.1.3. Competition regulations / official rules and criteria, English translation.
1.1.4. Official confirmation letter from organizer, English translation.
1.1.5. Media publications about beneficiary receiving the award, English translation.
1.1.6. Media publications about the award itself confirming national significance, English translation.

Principle:
One document cluster = one evidentiary episode or one legal argument.

Translations:
Every foreign-language document must have:
- original;
- English translation;
- translator certification, if used in the filing practice;
- explanatory note if context is not obvious.

Explanatory notes:
Use explanatory notes where the officer needs context:
- about an award;
- about an association;
- about a media outlet;
- about a company;
- about a competition/jury process;
- about a publication/journal;
- about salary data;
- about U.S. employer/company/work plan.

6. SECTION 1[B] - EB-1A CRITERIA
============================================================

[B] Evidence of eligibility (EB-1A Criteria)

Global rule for every criterion:
1) Start with a short legal/evidentiary summary.
2) List documents attached within the criterion.
3) For each evidentiary episode:
   - state the fact;
   - explain why it meets the regulatory criterion;
   - cite the document;
   - explain the authority/reputation/significance of the award/media/organization/event/source;
   - conclude.
4) End each criterion with a conclusion explicitly tracking the USCIS standard.

[B.i] Awards
------------------------------------------------------------

Heading:
[B.i] Evidence of receipt of nationally or internationally recognized prizes or awards for excellence.

POLICY MANUAL INSERT:
“First, USCIS determines if the person was the recipient of prizes or awards.”

POLICY MANUAL INSERT:
“Second, USCIS determines whether the award is a lesser nationally or internationally recognized prize or award which the person received for excellence in the field of endeavor.”

POLICY MANUAL INSERT:
“Relevant considerations regarding whether the basis for granting the prizes or awards was excellence in the field include, but are not limited to:
The criteria used to grant the awards or prizes;
The national or international significance of the awards or prizes in the field;
The number of awardees or prize recipients; and
Limitations on competitors.”

Drafting checklist:
- Did the beneficiary personally receive the award?
- Was it for excellence in the field?
- Is it nationally or internationally recognized?
- Is the award connected to the claimed specialization?
- Are rules/criteria included?
- Are the selection process and jury explained?
- Is media coverage or institutional recognition included?
- If team award: prove beneficiary was one of the recipients.

13. COMMON FAILURE MODES
============================================================

1. The petition claims too many criteria weakly.
Fix: Claim fewer criteria if the record is stronger. Three strong criteria plus strong final merits can be cleaner than eight criteria held together with office tape and optimism.

2. Awards section proves the award exists but not that it is nationally/internationally recognized.
Fix: Add rules, scale, organizer reputation, media, number of competitors, selection criteria, jury.

3. Membership section proves membership but not outstanding achievement.
Fix: Add membership level, admission standards, expert evaluation, and proof that payment/experience alone is insufficient.

4. Media section lists publications that are really PR.
Fix: Show independent editorial material about the person and their work. Include title, date, author, media metrics.

5. Judging section proves invitation but not actual judging.
Fix: Add evidence of completed reviews/evaluations/scores/protocols.

6. Contributions section proves originality but not major significance.
Fix: Add adoption, industry use, citations, licenses, media, awards, standards, corroborated expert letters.

7. Leading role section describes job duties but not distinguished organization.
Fix: Add company rankings, awards, media, clients, financials, funding, institutional recognition.

8. Salary section compares foreign salary to U.S. wages.
Fix: Compare to salary data for the relevant country/location and occupation, unless the salary is U.S.-based.

9. Section 2 sounds like NIW fan fiction.
Fix: Tie U.S. benefit to actual work plans, documents, company, contracts, role, industry need, and credible sources.

10. Final merits repeats all criteria.
Fix: Synthesize. Explain why the combined evidence shows sustained acclaim and top-of-field standing.
