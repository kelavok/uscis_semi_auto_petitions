EB-1A RFE RESPONSE TEMPLATE
LLM-FRIENDLY UNIFIED TEMPLATE
Based on several EB-1A RFE responses and standardized around the Serik Igbayev response structure.

Version: 2026-06-26
Purpose: create a consistent, shorter, adaptable template for preparing EB-1A responses to USCIS Requests for Evidence.
Final filing language: English.
Instruction language: Russian + English drafting blocks.
Primary structure: quote the RFE, respond directly, identify evidence already submitted, add new evidence, and explain why the criterion or issue is met.

================================================================================
0. HOW TO USE THIS TEMPLATE
================================================================================

Этот шаблон предназначен для LLM, которая готовит первый черновик ответа на RFE по EB-1A.

RFE Response не является обычной EB-1A petition. Его задача не заново рассказать биографию заявителя, а ответить на конкретные замечания USCIS. Поэтому LLM должна работать по матрице:

1. Что USCIS прямо признал met.
2. Что USCIS прямо признал not met.
3. Что USCIS оспорил по continued work.
4. Что USCIS оспорил по prospective substantial benefit.
5. Что USCIS оспорил по final merits determination.
6. Какие критерии не комментируются или имеют неопределенный статус.
7. Какие документы USCIS, возможно, не учел.
8. Какие новые документы приложены именно для ответа на RFE.
9. Какие старые документы нужно переслать повторно для удобства или потому что RFE их проигнорировал.

Главный формат каждого раздела:

[1] Heading: issue / criterion.
[2] Quote from RFE.
[3] Short procedural clarification: what exactly is disputed.
[4] Evidence map: resubmitted evidence + new evidence.
[5] Direct response to each RFE point.
[6] Legal/evidentiary explanation.
[7] Mini-conclusion.

Не писать абстрактно. Каждый тезис должен отвечать на конкретный дефект из RFE.

2. PRE-DRAFTING WORKFLOW FOR LLM
================================================================================

Before drafting the RFE response, complete the following internal steps.

STEP 1. Extract RFE metadata.
- Case number: [CASE_NO]
- Receipt / accepted for review date: [RECEIPT_DATE]
- RFE date: [RFE_DATE]
- Response date: [RESPONSE_DATE]
- Beneficiary: [BENEFICIARY_FULL_NAME]
- Area: [AREA]
- Specialization: [SPECIALIZATION]
- Petition type: EB-1A, Form I-140, INA §203(b)(1)(A)

STEP 2. Build the RFE issue matrix.

Use this table internally first. It may also be included in the response if it helps navigation.

| RFE issue no. | USCIS topic | RFE quote / summary | Status in RFE | Initial evidence already submitted | New evidence | Response strategy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Continued work | "[QUOTE]" | Not accepted / unclear | [Exhibit/page] | [Attachment/page] | Show prearranged commitments or concrete plan |
| 2 | Substantial benefit | "[QUOTE]" | Not accepted / unclear | [Exhibit/page] | [Attachment/page] | Explain prospective U.S. benefit through field-specific mechanism |
| 3 | Criterion [i] | "[QUOTE]" | Not accepted | [Exhibit/page] | [Attachment/page] | Address national/international recognition and excellence basis |
| 4 | Final merits | "[QUOTE]" | Not accepted | [Exhibit/page] | [Attachment/page] | Aggregate accepted criteria + sustained acclaim |

STEP 3. Classify every issue.

For each RFE issue, mark one of the following:
- ACCEPTED: USCIS expressly stated the criterion was met.
- NOT ACCEPTED: USCIS expressly found the criterion not met.
- UNCLEAR: RFE lists criterion as not met but gives no reasoning, or gives no remarks.
- IGNORED / OVERLOOKED: RFE says evidence was missing, but it was in the initial filing.
- GENERAL CONCERN: reliability, screenshots, links, translations, open sources, self-promotion, insufficient probative value.
- FINAL MERITS: USCIS accepts at least 3 criteria or may accept some criteria, but disputes whether evidence shows sustained acclaim / top of field.
- CONTINUED WORK: USCIS disputes U.S. work plans.
- SUBSTANTIAL BENEFIT: USCIS disputes prospective benefit to the United States.

STEP 4. Decide structure.

Default structure based on the most current standardized approach:

Cover Letter
Section A – Evidence of Intent to Continue Professional Activities in the United States
  A.1. Continued Work
  A.2. Prospective Substantial Benefit to the United States
Section B – Additional Evidence and Clarifications for the Criteria Claimed in the Petition
  B.[criterion number] – Criterion heading
Section C – Final Merits Determination, if challenged
Conclusion
Attachments / Appendix list
Technical separator pages

Alternative structure:
If RFE does not challenge continued work or substantial benefit, begin with a short "General Response Framework" and then criteria ordered by evidentiary strength.

Use the evidentiary-strength order when useful:
1. Criteria with strong objective documents.
2. Criteria where USCIS overlooked initial evidence.
3. Criteria with new evidence that directly cures RFE defects.
4. Weaker criteria or criteria acknowledged as uncertain.
5. Final merits.

Do not mechanically follow the original petition order if RFE strategy is better served by another order. Explain the ordering in the cover letter.

STEP 5. Evidence policy.

For each block, decide:
- Resubmit full evidence if USCIS appears to have overlooked it, if the RFE status is unclear, or if the evidence is central to the response.
- Refer to initial filing without resubmission if the evidence is voluminous and clearly already in the record.
- Add new evidence only to cure the exact RFE defect, not to create unrelated narrative bulk.

3. LEGAL STANDARD INSERTS
================================================================================

Use legal standards only where useful. Do not turn the RFE response into a law review article, because the officer already has a job and probably did not request recreational suffering.

3.1. EB-1A eligibility

Suggested drafting block:

The EB-1A classification requires evidence that the beneficiary has extraordinary ability in the sciences, arts, education, business, or athletics, demonstrated by sustained national or international acclaim, and that the beneficiary’s achievements have been recognized in the field through extensive documentation. The beneficiary must also intend to continue work in the area of extraordinary ability, and the beneficiary’s entry must substantially benefit the United States prospectively.

[LLM INSTRUCTION]
Use this in the introduction or final merits section only if the RFE challenges the overall statutory elements.

3.2. No job offer required, but continued work must be shown

Suggested drafting block:

Under 8 C.F.R. § 204.5(h)(5), neither a job offer nor labor certification is required for EB-1A classification. However, the petition must include clear evidence that the beneficiary is coming to the United States to continue work in the area of expertise. Such evidence may include letters from prospective employers, evidence of prearranged commitments, contracts, or a statement from the beneficiary detailing plans to continue work in the United States.

[LLM INSTRUCTION]
Use this in Section A.1 when RFE says the beneficiary has no prearranged commitments or no clear plans.

3.3. Two-step evidentiary review

Suggested drafting block:

USCIS applies a two-step analysis in EB-1A adjudication. First, USCIS determines whether the submitted evidence meets the regulatory criteria. Second, USCIS evaluates all evidence together in the final merits determination to determine whether the record establishes sustained national or international acclaim and that the beneficiary is among the small percentage who have risen to the top of the field.

[LLM INSTRUCTION]
Use this in the final merits section or when USCIS conflates initial-evidence analysis with final merits.

3.4. Preponderance of the evidence and totality

Suggested drafting block:

The evidence should be assessed under the preponderance of the evidence standard and in the context of the record as a whole. Where the record contains primary evidence, corroborating secondary evidence, and testimonial evidence, the probative value of these materials should be evaluated collectively rather than by isolating each document from the evidentiary context it supports.

[LLM INSTRUCTION]
Use this when RFE dismisses screenshots, letters, media, or private documents categorically.

- Keep the cover letter concise. The detailed argument belongs in the sections.

5. OPTIONAL SECTION 0 – GENERAL MATTERS AND EVIDENCE RELIABILITY
================================================================================

Use this section only if the RFE includes general concerns about:
- unreliable internet sources;
- screenshots;
- inaccessible links;
- open-source / user-edited platforms;
- insufficient translations;
- explanatory notes;
- private documents;
- self-serving letters;
- lack of probative value.

SECTION HEADING:

General Matters and Evidentiary Clarifications

As follows from the RFE:
“[INSERT EXACT RFE QUOTE ABOUT GENERAL RELIABILITY / INTERNET SOURCES / SCREENSHOTS / EVIDENCE QUALITY].”

In response, I respectfully provide the following clarification.

The evidence submitted with the initial petition and with this response is organized by evidentiary purpose. Primary evidence is used to establish the core facts under the applicable regulatory criteria, while secondary evidence is used to corroborate, contextualize, or demonstrate the broader public, professional, or institutional recognition of those facts.

I. Classification of Evidence

Documentary evidence includes:
- public documents, such as government records, official registrations, diplomas, or public institutional records;
- private documents, such as contracts, employer letters, award certificates, association records, correspondence, publisher confirmations, exhibition catalogues, and organizational records.

Testimonial evidence includes:
- letters from employers, supervisors, clients, collaborators, experts, organizers, publishers, or institutional representatives with direct knowledge of the beneficiary’s achievements, role, contribution, or recognition.

Secondary evidence includes:
- screenshots;
- online publications;
- media coverage;
- traffic data;
- rankings;
- web archives;
- photographs;
- explanatory notes;
- public source excerpts.

II. Primary and Secondary Evidence

Primary evidence directly establishes the factual claim. Examples include:
- award diploma and official confirmation from award organizer;
- membership certificate and bylaws;
- judging invitation and judging protocol;
- employment contract and employer letter;
- salary certificate and tax / pension / bank documents;
- publisher certificate confirming title, date, author, publication and editorial nature;
- intellectual property registration or implementation contract;
- exhibition catalogue and invitation.

Secondary evidence supports context. Examples include:
- media coverage showing public recognition of an award or exhibition;
- traffic or audience metrics showing that a publication is major media or major trade media;
- screenshots from official websites;
- rankings confirming the reputation of an organization, media outlet, award, or event;
- explanatory notes summarizing foreign institutions or documents unfamiliar to USCIS.

III. Screenshots and Website Links

Some website links may become unavailable due to website maintenance, domain changes, regional restrictions, content updates, or platform redesign. Temporary inaccessibility does not necessarily undermine the authenticity of the evidence if the record also contains screenshots, translations, publisher or organizer confirmations, archived materials, or other corroborating documentation.

The screenshots and online materials submitted in this response are used to support and contextualize primary evidence. They are not submitted as a substitute for primary documentation where primary documentation is available.

IV. Explanatory Notes

The explanatory notes are submitted to help interpret foreign documents, organizations, media outlets, awards, events, and professional contexts. They are not presented as independent primary evidence unless expressly supported by cited source documents. Their function is explanatory and navigational.

V. Conclusion for General Matters

Accordingly, the record should be assessed as a whole. The key facts are supported by primary documents, while secondary materials provide context regarding public recognition, professional significance, audience reach, organizational reputation, or field relevance.

[LLM INSTRUCTIONS]
- Do not include this section in every RFE. Use it only when RFE attacks reliability or evidence type.
- Do not overdefend screenshots. Explain their purpose.
- If RFE says open-source platform = unreliable, distinguish between website software and editorial reliability.
- If RFE says links are inaccessible, attach screenshots, archived copies, official confirmations, and translations where possible.
- If RFE attacks explanatory notes, say they are context, not primary proof.

[LLM INSTRUCTIONS]

- For startup cases, show the company is operational enough: registration + IP + product + market engagement.
- For employment cases, show employer legitimacy + role duties + field alignment.
- If RFE says "no job offer," remind that EB-1A does not require a job offer but requires clear evidence of continued work.

A.2. Prospective Substantial Benefit to the United States
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

HEADING:
A.2. My Entry Will Substantially Benefit Prospectively the United States

As follows from the RFE:
“[INSERT EXACT RFE QUOTE ABOUT SUBSTANTIAL BENEFIT].”

My work directly addresses [identify concrete U.S. problem / market need / institutional need / industry inefficiency]. The prospective benefit to the United States follows from the mechanism by which my work will [improve / develop / commercialize / scale / teach / create / implement] [specific result].

Use one of the following benefit theories:

1. Economic benefit:
My work will contribute to [business growth / productivity / cost reduction / job creation / tax base / capital allocation / U.S. competitiveness] through [specific mechanism].

2. Innovation / technology benefit:
My work introduces [methodology / technology / platform / framework] that enables [industry users / companies / institutions] to [specific function].

3. Regulatory / institutional benefit:
My work supports [compliance / transparency / auditability / reporting / governance / safety / quality] in [field], which is relevant to U.S. institutions because [reason].

4. Labor market / workforce benefit:
My work helps [employers / workers / institutions] address [skills gaps / training / workforce valuation / retention / productivity] through [specific method].

5. Cultural / artistic / design benefit:
My work will contribute to [U.S. cultural sector / design innovation / architectural practice / exhibitions / public recognition / professional exchange] through [specific U.S. activity].

6. Education / knowledge transfer:
My work will benefit U.S. [students / professionals / organizations] through [teaching / workshops / publications / professional training / methodology dissemination].

Suggested drafting block:

The prospective benefit to the United States is both practical and field-specific. First, [BENEFIT 1]. Second, [BENEFIT 2]. Third, [BENEFIT 3]. Finally, because [U.S. company / employer / project / collaboration] is located in the United States, the development and implementation of this work will occur within the U.S. professional ecosystem.

Endorsed / validated by relevant experts:
The practical relevance of my work is also supported by [letters / correspondence / statements / pilot interest] from [recognized U.S. experts / industry leaders / academic specialists / investors / institutions]. These materials show that my U.S. work has been reviewed by individuals with relevant expertise and is not merely speculative.

Conclusion:
In sum, my work addresses [problem] by introducing [solution]. Given [scale of field / U.S. market relevance / regulatory need / professional demand], my continued work in the United States will substantially benefit the United States prospectively.

[LLM INSTRUCTIONS]
- Do not import NIW-style "national importance" language mechanically. EB-1A asks whether entry will substantially benefit the U.S. prospectively.
- It is acceptable to explain the practical importance of the specialization, but connect it to the applicant’s own work.
- Avoid saying "substantial benefit" just because the field is useful. Show how this beneficiary’s entry creates the benefit.
- Use reports and third-party sources only where they support the problem statement.
- Use expert letters or correspondence where they support U.S. demand, relevance, implementation, or scale.
- For startup cases, link benefit to product execution in the U.S.
- For employment cases, link benefit to the proposed role and U.S. employer’s work.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Criterion (i): Nationally or Internationally Recognized Prizes or Awards for Excellence
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Use when RFE says:
- award is not nationally/internationally recognized;
- award did not recognize excellence;
- recipient not proven;
- organizer not official;
- website links unreliable;
- no media coverage;
- award recognition comes only from awarding entity;
- no direct URL or confirmation from organizer.

RFE response checklist:
1. Prove the beneficiary received the award.
2. Prove the award is in the field.
3. Prove the award was for excellence.
4. Prove national or international recognition.
5. Prove selection was competitive / merit-based.
6. Prove organizer, jury, criteria, and official source.
7. If requested, provide direct URLs and organizer correspondence.
8. Use media coverage and rankings to show award visibility.

Suggested headings:
A. The record directly confirms that I received the award.
B. The award relates to my field of [FIELD].
C. The award is granted for excellence, not participation.
D. The award has national / international recognition.
E. The evidence includes official organizer confirmation and direct URLs.
F. Conclusion.

Drafting block:

The RFE states:
“[QUOTE].”

In response, I provide the following primary evidence confirming receipt of the award: [award diploma], [official organizer letter], [official winners page], and [award regulations]. The organizer letter confirms that I was the recipient of [AWARD] in the category [CATEGORY] on [DATE]. The award regulations show that winners are selected based on [criteria], which directly concern excellence in [FIELD].

The award’s national / international recognition is supported by [media publications / audience data / rankings / history of award / number of participants / geographic scope / jury composition / institutional partners]. These materials demonstrate recognition beyond the issuing organization.

Conclusion:
The evidence establishes that [BENEFICIARY] received a nationally / internationally recognized award for excellence in [FIELD]. Therefore, Criterion (i) is satisfied.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Criterion (ii): Membership in Associations Requiring Outstanding Achievement
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Use when RFE says:
- membership is general;
- based on fee, education, years of experience, or employment;
- no evidence recognized experts judged outstanding achievement;
- wrong membership level;
- association not in field.

Checklist:
1. Prove membership.
2. Identify membership level.
3. Prove association is in the field.
